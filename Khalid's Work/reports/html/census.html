Technical note

Benchmarking HIES 2024-25 against the 2023 Census

Households, population, rural share and provinces

SurveyHIES 2024-25, 30,123 households interviewed Oct 2024 – Jun 2025
Benchmark7th Population and Housing Census, 1 March 2023
CoverageFour provinces and Islamabad (AJK and Gilgit-Baltistan in neither)
DateNovember 2026

Summary

  • Households: HIES gives 40.1 million, within 1.3% of the census figure brought forward to the survey period.
  • Population: HIES gives 239.8 million, about 5% below the census figure brought forward, because HIES households are smaller (5.98 people against 6.30).
  • Rural share and provinces: agree with the census to within one percentage point.

1How the comparison works

  • HIES totals. Each of the 30,123 surveyed households has a weight: the number of Pakistani households it stands for. Adding up the weights gives the number of households in Pakistan. Adding up weight × household members gives the population.
  • Same point in time. The census counted Pakistan in March 2023; HIES interviews were about two years later (middle: February 2025). The census counts are therefore also grown to February 2025 at the census's own yearly growth rates: 3.1% for households and 2.55% for population.

The number of households agrees. HIES counts 40.1 million households, 4.8% more than the census found in March 2023. That is about two years of growth: compared with the census grown to February 2025, HIES is only 1.3% lower.

The population is lower than expected. HIES finds the same population as the 2023 census (239.8 against 241.5 million), but by 2025 the population should have grown to about 254 million. The reason is household size: HIES records 5.98 people per household, the census 6.30. HIES counts only usual household members, does not cover people living in hostels, barracks or prisons, and, like most surveys, tends to miss some very large households.

Islamabad is identified from the first digit of the HIES area code (the raw files code it as Punjab). The census releases consulted do not give households by province.

Each province's share of the population is within 0.1 percentage points of the census, and each province's rural share within 0.8 points. This is expected, as the HIES sample was drawn from the census, and confirms that the weights are applied correctly.

4Conclusions

  • HIES reproduces the census picture of where people live: by province and between rural and urban areas.
  • The number of households (40.1 million) is the most reliable HIES total. This supports using the household as the unit of analysis.
  • Population totals from HIES are about 5% too low for 2025 and should be quoted with that caveat.
